Library

Library

Every source you’re reading, in one place, kept exactly as it came.

Why I built it this way

I keep each file once, exactly as it came, so every citation leads back to the real page.

Collections are yours to define: a class, a semester, a piece of work. There’s no set structure, and one document can sit in as many collections as it belongs to, because real reading doesn’t fit in one folder.

The library lives on your computer, so your reading list is nobody’s business but yours.

Knowledge

Knowledge

Everything you’ve noticed and named, across all your sources, on one page.

Why I built it this way

Learning is noticing and naming things yourself, so highlighting and filing are one step. The habit sticks while you read.

The four kinds match how ideas show up in reading: a framework has parts, a formula has variables, a key term has a meaning, and data is a picture.

Each item keeps its own copy of the passage and where it came from, and opens at the exact passage. Highlights you’ve given a type stay even if you delete the document they came from.

Quotes you can trust

A PDF has a layer of text hidden behind the page, and that text can be wrong while the page looks right. So every passage you take from a PDF is checked against the page itself. Anything doubtful is marked “Check this” until you’ve looked. It can still miss a missing accent, so check your quotes too.

Projects

Projects

Where you write, with your Knowledge beside the text.

Why I built it this way

A project holds only what you bring into it, so a source you reuse from earlier work doesn’t arrive with dozens of old notes.

Every citation can be opened at its passage.

References are honest rather than automatic. No tool reads every source correctly by itself, so CiteSavant never guesses: anything missing shows as an amber placeholder, such as [year].
