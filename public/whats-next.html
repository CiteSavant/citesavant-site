What’s next

What I’m working on

CiteSavant is an early version. This is what I’m working on now, in no fixed order and with no dates.

A calmer, more consistent look

Every screen is being redesigned around one shared layout, so the app feels like one place.

Filling in a source’s details faster

Select the words on a source’s page (its title, authors or year) and drop them straight into its details, instead of typing them.

Better reading of business case studies

Starting with Harvard Business School cases, so their details are suggested correctly for you to confirm.

One library on two computers

Keep your library folder in a place both computers can reach, safely, so you can move between them.

AI that helps, never writes for you

An optional assistant that looks at the parts of your library and knowledge you choose to share with it, and suggests things: sources worth using for a project, passages that support a point, gaps in your references. It will not write your paper.

How it is kept in check

  • It is off until you switch it on, and uses your own key with an AI provider (which may charge you; CiteSavant stays free).
  • Before anything is sent, you see exactly what will be sent, and nothing goes until you press send.
  • It only suggests. Every suggestion is marked as one until you accept it, and nothing enters your library or your project without you.
  • It can’t act on its own: it can’t open, change or delete anything, and its replies are checked and shown as plain text.
  • Nothing is sent to me, ever.

Every new feature follows the same rules: your data stays yours, and nothing is filled in without you.

Good to know

  • CiteSavant is an early test version.
  • It works best in Chrome or Edge on a computer.
  • Check every quotation and citation against your source before you submit.
